# Why Nashboard

A plain-language page for anyone who has never used a smart home. No jargon, and nothing here asks you to learn anything.

## The problem

A modern house is full of things that know something: the weather service knows rain is coming, the garage door knows it is
open, the doorbell knows a package arrived, the thermostat knows it is cold out. But that knowledge is scattered across a dozen
apps, each with its own screen, its own login and its own alerts. Most of the time you don't look at any of them, and then you
miss the one thing that mattered: the garage open all night, the laundry left out when the rain started.

The usual fix is a control panel full of switches, graphs and settings. That just trades a dozen apps for one busy screen
that you have to check and keep tidy.

## What it does for you

Nashboard pulls the useful facts from all those places into one calm screen, and shows only the ones that matter right now.

- It watches things so you don't have to.
- When something needs attention, a card appears. When the situation is over, the card goes away.
- It is the same screen on a kitchen tablet, a phone, a laptop or a TV, so everyone in the house sees the same picture.
- It is made for the whole family, not only the person who set it up.

## You don't have to do anything

This is the main point. There is nothing to operate, schedule or maintain day to day.

- **Cards show up on their own.** Rain coming, the garage left open, a package waiting outside, the dog's timer: each appears
  when it is relevant.
- **They go away on their own.** Once the rain has passed or the door is closed, the card clears itself.
- **You can use it or ignore it.** A card is information, not a chore. Glance at it, act on it, or carry on with your day.
- **Nothing breaks if you never touch it.** There are no routines to keep up and no buttons you must press.

If you do want to tinker, there are settings, but they are optional.

## A day with it

- **Morning:** you glance at the kitchen screen while the coffee brews. It shows the weather, who is home and what is on
  the calendar, with nothing else in the way.
- **Before leaving:** a card says rain is coming this afternoon. You grab an umbrella. You didn't check a weather app.
- **Mid-morning:** a card shows a package was left at the door. You bring it in when it suits you, and the card disappears.
- **Afternoon:** the dog is in a timeout and a card shows the timer counting down. When it ends, the card is gone.
- **Evening:** a card points out the garage is still open. You close it, or confirm that you want it closed, and the card clears.
- **Night:** the screen dims and goes quiet. A freeze warning appears only on the nights it actually matters.

## What it isn't

- **Not a control panel you must manage.** You are not expected to configure it, watch it or keep it running.
- **Not a pile of notifications.** It prefers a quiet card on a screen you were already looking at over a loud alert.
- **Not something that acts behind your back.** Anything physical, like a lock or the garage, asks you to confirm first.
- **Not only for tech people.** If you can read a screen, you can use it.
